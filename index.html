A fee checklist for crypto debit cards

Last updated 23 November 2026. Not financial, tax, or legal advice.

Look past “0% transaction fees.” The cost of using a crypto debit card usually sits in conversion, ATM, and issuance charges as well.

Many crypto debit cards are advertised as “fee-free.” That phrase usually describes one line on the price list, not the full cost of using the card for a year. Before you load funds, map every layer that can take a cut.

The three layers that actually matter

CryptoCardsLab’s fee comparison groups charges into three layers. You can use the same structure when you read any issuer’s schedule:

  1. Transaction fees — a percentage or flat amount per purchase. This is the number most landing pages highlight, often as “0%”.
  2. Conversion spread — the markup on turning crypto into the currency you spend. Issuers may call this a spread, an FX fee, or a liquidation fee. It is easy to miss because it is not always shown as a separate line at checkout.
  3. Fixed and conditional fees — issuance or delivery, inactivity, ATM withdrawals, replacements, and similar extras. These often live in a PDF rather than on a comparison table.

A card that looks cheap on layer 1 can still be expensive on layer 2 if you spend in a currency that is not the card’s default. ATM withdrawals can add both a fee and, in some programmes, an extra identity check.

Do not use a shutdown product as a “cheap” option. The Gnosis Pay consumer card and web app end at 23:59 UTC on 20 December 2026, so it should not be part of a lowest-cost shortlist. CryptoCardsLab’s fee calculator leaves it out of the lowest-cost highlight for that reason.

How to compare costs without guessing

Pick a realistic monthly spend, then add every layer — not just the purchase fee. CryptoCardsLab’s calculator lets you set a monthly amount and compare living cards. The long-form crypto debit card fees guide explains the three layers and points to official issuer fee pages used for the issuer columns.

If you mostly spend a stablecoin such as USDC, read the USDC debit card guide as well. Conversion can still apply when the card settles in a different currency, and identity checks still apply to the programmes reviewed there.

A short fee checklist

  • Write down your typical monthly spend and the currencies you actually pay in.
  • Find the official fee PDF or help article, not a screenshot from a social post.
  • List issuance or delivery charges for virtual and physical cards separately.
  • Check FX or conversion rules for non-default currencies, including any small-transaction minimums.
  • Check ATM fees and whether an ATM withdrawal can trigger another review.
  • Ignore cashback maths until the fees are clear. A reward rate cannot be judged in isolation.

This is a short, independently written checklist. For full reviews, official fee tables, and source notes, use the linked pages on the destination site. Figures and issuer rules change — confirm them on the issuer’s own site before you apply or claim.
